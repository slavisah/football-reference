# Copa América

Copa América began in 1916 and is the oldest still-running continental men's national-team competition. It was known as the South American Championship until 1975.

Its history is less uniform than the World Cup or EURO. Some editions used a final league table, some were played over several months, some had no fixed host, and invited teams from outside South America have participated since 1993.

## Important editorial warning

This page intentionally begins with champions and runners-up rather than claiming that one table schema fits every edition. The per-edition "Format" column - audited 2026-08-02 (intensive run) against secondary sources (see `docs/SOURCES.md`), one of:

- **League table**: champion decided outright by round-robin standings (the pre-1975 South American Championship's normal shape; also 1989 and 1991, which added a group stage ahead of a final round-robin group rather than a single match);
- **Final playoff**: the league table finished level on points and an extra decider match was played (1919, 1922, 1937, 1949, 1953);
- **Home-and-away**: no fixed host; the final was played over two legs, sometimes with a third decider match (1975, 1979, 1983);
- **Knockout final**: group stage followed by a single-elimination bracket ending in a one-off final (1987, and every edition from 1993 onward except 2016);
- **Special centenary edition**: the 2016 Copa América Centenario, an extra edition outside the normal cycle marking the tournament's 100th anniversary.

Third and fourth places are still **not** added to every edition - that would need a separate per-tournament audit (many early editions had no such placing at all, since the format was a single round-robin table), so it remains open for a future pass.

## Memorable moments

- Argentina, Brazil, Chile, and Uruguay played the first edition in 1916.
- Bolivia won its only title as host in 1963.
- Colombia won its first title in 2001.
- Chile won its first two titles in consecutive tournaments, 2015 and 2016.
- Argentina moved ahead as the competition's most successful team by winning in 2024.
