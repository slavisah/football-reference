# Men's Ballon d'Or

France Football created the Ballon d'Or in 1956. Eligibility rules changed over time: the award was initially limited to European players, opened to players of any nationality at European clubs in 1995, and became fully global in 2007. From 2010 through 2015 it was presented jointly with FIFA.

## Important editorial note

The **Ceremony date** column (added 2026-08-03, intensive run) records the calendar date each year's winner was announced, for the "On this day in football history" widget on the home page - the same widget already covers the FIFA World Cup, UEFA EURO, Copa América and UEFA Nations League. Unlike those four tournaments, the Ballon d'Or has no match to date; the value recorded here is the France Football magazine announcement/ceremony date each year (1956-2015), or the gala date from 2016 onward.

Two sourcing notes worth flagging:

- **1956-2009**: every year was announced through a France Football magazine issue, so the "ceremony date" is that issue's cover date. Two years have a genuine source conflict rather than a single clean answer: **1965** (28 December per Wikipedia and the weekly-Tuesday-publication pattern, vs. 27 December implied by one club retrospective) and **1973** (25 December per Wikipedia and the same pattern, vs. one other source giving 31 December). Both were resolved in favor of the date consistent with France Football's known weekly Tuesday publication schedule in that era. Four further years (1976, 1978, 1984, 1986) rest on a single corroborating source rather than two independent ones, despite a genuine search effort for a second. All of this research relied on WebSearch summaries rather than direct page fetches - this environment's outbound network policy blocks direct requests to Wikipedia, RSSSF and similar hosts - so a future pass with direct source access could usefully re-verify these six years specifically.
- **2010-2015**: presented jointly with FIFA as the "FIFA Ballon d'Or" at a Zurich gala. Every one of these ceremonies was held in **January of the year after** the award-year label (e.g. the "2010" award was presented on 10 January 2011) - the table's Year column intentionally keeps the award-year label throughout (matching how every other column already treats that year), while the Ceremony date cell records the real calendar date the gala took place, exactly as EURO 2020's Final date column already records 2021 for its postponed final.
- **2020** keeps "—" for Ceremony date, same as its "Not awarded"/"—" Winner and National team cells - no ceremony took place that year, which is itself the historical fact, not a research gap.

## Notes

- Lev Yashin remains the only goalkeeper to win the men's award.
- George Weah became the first African winner in 1995.
- Luka Modrić won in 2018.
- The 2020 award was cancelled.
- Ousmane Dembélé won the 2025 award.
